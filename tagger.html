Work / Tagger prototype

A transaction tagger that learns

Bank statements often print a company's registered name, not its brand: Swiggy appears as BUNDL TECHNOLOGIES, Ola as ANI TECHNOLOGIES. This working prototype starts with keyword rules, asks an AI model about the merchants those miss, and turns each of your corrections into a rule for that merchant, past and future.

    How it works

    Three layers, cheapest first

    Each layer only sees what the cheaper one before it couldn't place, and your corrections always win.

    1. Keyword rules firstCheap and predictable: SWIGGY, UBER, NETFLIX. They get 11 of 24 October transactions right (46%), and some wrong: “INNOVATIVE RETAIL CONCEPTS” matches RETAIL and lands in Shopping, but it's BigBasket, which is Groceries.
    2. An AI model for the legal namesOnly merchants the keywords can't place go to the model, with a fixed list of categories and an instruction to answer “Untagged” when unsure. It returns a short reason, shown under the transaction.
    3. Your corrections become rulesChange a category and it asks whether to apply it to every transaction from that merchant, including future ones. That's the step that compounds.

    What I'd want to learn from real users

    Whether people trust an AI reason enough to leave a tag alone.

    And how many corrections it takes before Untagged stops being their biggest category. The order of layers matters too: user rules always win over the model, so a correction is never overridden.
