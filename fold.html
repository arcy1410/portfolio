The short version

  • Problem

    Fold shows refresh status in a small pill, but balances and totals carry no date. A stale account looks current.

  • What I'd ship

    After 3 days without a refresh: one alert, dated totals, a free retry and a reconnect option.

  • How I'd know

    Days to notice under 3; more stale accounts recovering within 14 days, with no rise in opt-outs.

Evidence

What 50 reviews complain about

Fold is well liked: 4.6 stars from about 1,400 reviews, so its complaints show where it falls short. Bank sync drew only five reviews, but every one was serious.

Fold tracks bank accounts, cards and investments in one app, pulling data through India's Account Aggregator network. I took 50 reviews from the 160 newest on Google Play. Only 24 of the 160 were rated 1 to 3 stars, so I kept all 24 and added the 16 newest 4-star and 10 newest 5-star reviews. For each I logged a summary, tags, Fold's reply and helpful votes, then rated severity: blocker (can't use Fold, or leaving), wrong numbers (stale, duplicated, miscategorised), or friction (everything else).

Pricing drew the most agreement: 51 helpful votes, 33 of them on a single review. Five reviews asked for features Fold already has. In reviewers' words:

  • Bank syncit's just like old passbook now
  • Pricing₹299/month is a ridiculous price
  • Categoriesbig basket purchases is noted as innovative film and tagged as entertainment
  • Hard to findi never knew that we can enter transaction manually
  • Getting inthe app closes as soon as I open it

Decision

Choosing one fix, then checking it twice

I scored each theme on three tests: is it common, does it hurt, and can Fold act on it? My first two picks already existed in the app, so the fix narrowed to the real gap.

ThemeCommonHurtsFold can actTotal
Wrong or rigid categories2338

Gaps in what Fold tracks3227
My first pick already existed, so I checked before committing.

The top-scoring theme was wrong categories, and my fix was to let a tag change apply to the merchant's other transactions. Checking in the app showed Fold already does this. I moved to the runner-up, bank sync, then checked again: Fold already shows each bank's refresh status in a pill and a sheet. So the fix narrowed to the real gap: the numbers people read carry no date.

Every sync review was serious: three blockers and two cases of wrong numbers. SBI appears in 4 of the 5, and Fold's own docs say refresh failures are very common at several large banks. Fold can't fix a bank's feed, but it fully controls when and how users find out. Pricing is the loudest complaint but is mostly about price, a deliberate business choice.

One-page PRD

The spec, on one page

The first version does five things: detect, tell, explain, offer a next step, and clear itself.

PRD

Warn users when a bank goes quiet

Fold, bank sync. The first version, on one page.

Author
Ankur Roy Chowdhury
Status
Draft for review
Date
31 Oct 2026
Evidence
50 Play Store reviews

Problem

Fold refreshes bank data once a day. When a bank's feed fails, the account quietly stops updating; one reviewer's SBI account went over a month. Users find out only when a balance looks wrong.

1×a day, scheduled refresh1 month+longest stale account5 of 5sync reviews serious

User and need

Anyone with a connected bank, most often at banks whose feeds fail. They open Fold to check where their money stands, for example before a large purchase. They need to know:

  • when a number is out of date,
  • why it stopped updating,
  • and what they can do about it.

Scope

In the first version
  • Detect a stale account
  • Tell the user
  • Explain why
  • Offer a next step
  • Clear itself
Later
  • Statement upload to fill the gap
Not in scope
  • Fixing banks' feeds, which only banks can do

Requirements

IDRequirementDetailRelease
R1DetectMark an account stale after 3 days without a successful refresh, respecting known bank patterns (some don't refresh on weekends).v1
R2TellOne push notification when an account goes stale, one reminder at 7 days, a banner on the account, and any total that includes it marked with the date of its oldest data.v1
R3ExplainName the bank, the last successful refresh, and the cause when Fold knows it.v1
R4Offer a next stepA retry that doesn't count against the 5 extra refreshes a month free users get, and a way to reconnect.v1
R5Clear itselfRemove the flag on the next successful refresh; let users mute alerts for one account.v1
R6Fill the gapStatement upload, so a stale account can be brought up to date by hand.Later

How it plays out

  1. Day 0Last successful refresh
  2. Day 3Marked stale: push alert, banner, dated totals
  3. Day 7One reminder, then no more
  4. Any timeFree retry, or reconnect
  5. Next refreshThe flag clears itself

Success metrics

MainDays to noticeMedian days from last successful refresh to the owner opening the warning3 or fewer
SecondaryRecoveryShare of stale accounts refreshing again within 7 days of the warningSet after a 2-week baseline
OutcomeRetention60-day retention of users who had an account go staleHigher than pre-launch
GuardrailFalse alarmsWarnings for accounts that refresh on their own within a dayUnder 10%
GuardrailMutesShare of warned users who mute alerts for an accountNo steady rise

Open questions

  • Is 3 days the right threshold at every bank?
  • How many stale accounts come from expired Account Aggregator consent rather than a failing bank?
  • Does Fold already send a push alert? My banks were all refreshing, so I couldn't check.

Launch

Test before launch: a two-group A/B test on users with a connected bank, with recovery as the primary metric. Ship to everyone only if recovery goes up and the guardrails hold.

A/B test plan

How I'd know it works

Recovery is the primary metric: more stale accounts updating again within 14 days, with opt-outs and uninstalls as guardrails.

Hypothesis

IfFold alerts users once an account goes 3 days without updating, thenmore stale accounts will be updating again within 14 days, becauseusers retry or reconnect instead of going weeks without noticing.

Design

Group A: today's app
  • No alert and no dated totals
  • Log the moment an alert would have fired, so both groups start the clock at the same point
Group B: the fix
  • The alert, and one reminder at 7 days
  • Dated totals and the account banner
  • The free retry and reconnect

Split users with a connected bank into two equal groups at random, by user rather than account, so each person sees one consistent app.

Metrics

PrimaryRecoveryShare of stale accounts updating again within 14 days
SecondaryFix actionsShare of users who retry or reconnect within 7 days
SecondaryTime to recoveryMedian days from going stale to the next successful refresh
Outcome60-day retentionShare still opening Fold 60 days after their account went stale
GuardrailNotification opt-outsShare who turn off Fold's notifications within 14 days
GuardrailUninstallsShare who uninstall within 14 days

Sample size: try the assumptions

Fold doesn't publish how often stale accounts recover, so I assumed half do within 14 days today. 95% confidence, 80% power.

385per group

To spot a lift from 50% to 60%, I'd need about 390 users with a stale account per group.

n = (1.96 + 0.84)² × [0.50×0.50 + 0.60×0.40] ÷ 0.10² ≈ 385

Timing

Enroll for 2 full weeks or moreSome banks don't refresh on weekends
Then follow each user for 14 daysThe first result comes after about four weeks

Fix the sample size up front and don't stop early because the numbers look good.

What I'd decide

Ship to everyone

Risks

One bank's outage can swamp the result, so check that each bank's users are split evenly and report by bank. If two weeks don't produce 390 per group, run longer rather than accept a smaller sample.

Limits

What this can't tell you

Reviews come mostly from unhappy users, so they show what hurts, not how many people it hurts.

The sample is Play Store only, and the tags and severity ratings are one reader's calls; four themes sit within one point. Next, I'd interview five users whose bank went stale, to learn how they found out and what they did.
