Work / Fold

Fold: warn users when a bank goes quiet

I read 50 Play Store reviews of Fold, a money tracker I use, and found one problem worth fixing first: when a bank's feed fails, balances keep looking current for weeks. This page takes it from review analysis to a one-page PRD and an A/B test plan.

Product
Fold (money tracking, India)
Evidence
50 reviews, Feb–Oct 2026
Deliverables
Analysis, PRD, A/B plan
Date
28–31 Oct 2026

  • Problem

    Fold shows refresh status in a small pill, but balances and totals carry no date. A stale account looks current.

  • What I'd ship

    After 3 days without a refresh: one alert, dated totals, a free retry and a reconnect option.

  • How I'd know

    Days to notice under 3; more stale accounts recovering within 14 days, with no rise in opt-outs.

1. What users complain about

Fold tracks bank accounts, cards and investments in one app, pulling data through India's Account Aggregator network. It's well liked: 4.6 stars on Google Play from about 1,400 reviews. Complaints about a product people like show where it falls short, not whether it works.

I took 50 reviews from the 160 newest on Google Play. Only 24 of the 160 were rated 1 to 3 stars, so I kept all 24 and added the 16 newest 4-star and 10 newest 5-star reviews. For each I logged a summary, tags, Fold's reply and helpful votes, then rated severity: blocker (can't use Fold, or leaving), wrong numbers (stale, duplicated, miscategorised), or friction (everything else).

Pricing drew the most agreement: 51 helpful votes, 33 of them on a single review. Five reviews asked for features Fold already has. In reviewers' words:

Pricing“₹299/month is a ridiculous price”
Categories“big basket purchases is noted as innovative film and tagged as entertainment”
Hard to find“i never knew that we can enter transaction manually”
Getting in“the app closes as soon as I open it”

Choosing one fix

I scored each theme on three tests: is it common, does it hurt, and can Fold act on it?

ThemeCommonHurtsFold can actTotal
Wrong or rigid categories2338
Bank sync breaks2327
Paywall and pricing3137
Gaps in what Fold tracks3227
My first pick already existed, so I checked before committing.The top-scoring theme was wrong categories, and my fix was to let a tag change apply to the merchant's other transactions. Checking in the app showed Fold already does this. I moved to the runner-up, bank sync, then checked again: Fold already shows each bank's refresh status in a pill and a sheet. So the fix narrowed to the real gap, which is that the numbers people read carry no date.

Every sync review was serious: three blockers and two cases of wrong numbers. SBI appears in 4 of the 5, and Fold's own docs say refresh failures are very common at several large banks. Fold can't fix a bank's feed, but it fully controls when and how users find out. Pricing is the loudest complaint but is mostly about price, a deliberate business choice.

2. One-page PRD

Problem. Fold refreshes bank data once a day. When a bank's feed fails, the account quietly stops updating; one reviewer's SBI account went over a month. Users find out only when a balance looks wrong.

User. Anyone with a connected bank, most often at banks whose feeds fail. They open Fold to check where their money stands, for example before a large purchase. They need to know when a number is out of date, why, and what they can do about it.

The first version does five things:

  1. Detect. Mark an account stale after 3 days without a successful refresh, respecting known bank patterns (some don't refresh on weekends).
  2. Tell. One push notification when an account goes stale, one reminder at 7 days, a banner on the account, and any total that includes it marked with the date of its oldest data.
  3. Explain. Name the bank, the last successful refresh, and the cause when Fold knows it.
  4. Offer a next step. A retry that doesn't count against the 5 extra refreshes a month free users get, and a way to reconnect.
  5. Clear itself. Remove the flag on the next successful refresh; let users mute alerts for one account.

Later: statement upload to fill the gap. Not in scope: fixing banks' feeds, which only banks can do.

MetricDefinitionStarting target
Days to notice (main)Median days from last successful refresh to the owner opening the warning3 or fewer
RecoveryShare of stale accounts refreshing again within 7 days of the warningSet after a 2-week baseline
Retention (outcome)60-day retention of users who had an account go staleHigher than pre-launch
False alarms (guardrail)Warnings for accounts that refresh on their own within a dayUnder 10%
Mutes (guardrail)Share of warned users who mute alerts for an accountNo steady rise

Open questions: is 3 days the right threshold at every bank? How many stale accounts come from expired Account Aggregator consent rather than a failing bank? Does Fold already send a push alert? My banks were all refreshing, so I couldn't check.

3. A/B test plan

Hypothesis. If Fold alerts users once an account goes 3 days without updating, more stale accounts will be updating again within 14 days, because users retry or reconnect instead of going weeks without noticing.

Design. Split users with a connected bank into two equal groups at random, by user rather than account, so each person sees one consistent app. Group A keeps today's app; group B gets the alert, dated totals, the free retry and reconnect. In group A, log the moment an alert would have fired, so both groups start the clock at the same point.

MetricRoleDefinition
RecoveryPrimaryShare of stale accounts updating again within 14 days
Fix actionsSecondaryShare of users who retry or reconnect within 7 days
Time to recoverySecondaryMedian days from going stale to the next successful refresh
60-day retentionOutcomeShare still opening Fold 60 days after their account went stale
Notification opt-outsGuardrailShare who turn off Fold's notifications within 14 days
UninstallsGuardrailShare who uninstall within 14 days

Sample size. Fold doesn't publish how often stale accounts recover, so I assumed half do within 14 days today. Spotting a lift from 50% to 60% at 95% confidence and 80% power needs about 390 users with a stale account per group:

n = (1.96 + 0.84)² × [0.5×0.5 + 0.6×0.4] ÷ 0.1² ≈ 385 per group

Enroll for at least two full weeks, since some banks don't refresh on weekends, then follow each user for 14 days: the first result comes after about four weeks. Fix the sample size up front and don't stop early because the numbers look good.

RecoveryGuardrailsDecision
UpFlatShip to everyone
UpWorseCut the noise first (fewer reminders or a later threshold), then retest
Not upFlatLook by cause; keep alerts only where users can fix the problem
Not upWorseDon't ship the push alert; keep only the in-app flag

Risks. One bank's outage can swamp the result, so check that each bank's users are split evenly and report by bank. If two weeks don't produce 390 per group, run longer rather than accept a smaller sample.

Limits

Reviews come mostly from unhappy users, so they show what hurts, not how many people it hurts. The sample is Play Store only, and the tags and severity ratings are one reader's calls; four themes sit within one point. Next, I'd interview five users whose bank went stale, to learn how they found out and what they did.
