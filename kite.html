Opening a Zerodha Kite account: 19 screens in 7 minutes

I opened a real Zerodha account, from the Kite app's welcome screen to my first watchlist. Pre-filled KYC details made it fast. The friction came from a step counter that undercounts, five hand-offs between the app, the browser and an e-sign site, and setup that carries on after “Congratulations”.

Product
Zerodha Kite, iPhone
Run
31 Oct 2026, 11:28 to 11:35
Evidence
Screenshot of every screen
Deliverable
Journey map and fixes

  • Problem

    “5 steps” covers 9 of 19 screens; 8 screens come after “One last step!”

  • Fixes

    An honest progress bar, one place to sign up, and a beginner-safe default for F&O.

  • Measure

    Completion at every screen, and time from opening the app to the first watchlist.

The journey

Each row is one screen, placed where it happened: the Kite app, Zerodha's sign-up site in the browser, the Digio e-sign site, or Kite on the web.

Kite's “5 steps” cover 9 of the 19 screens. The rest come before the counter starts or after it says the last step is done.

Where the flow slowed me down

Six moments added effort or doubt, roughly in order of how much they cost.

What Kite gets right

For a regulated brokerage account, 7 minutes is fast, and most of the flow is well judged.

  • KYC does the typing. Once I entered my PAN, my address came from existing KYC records, so I picked it instead of typing it.
  • Plain words at anxious moments. “We will not send you any physical documents unless you ask us to.”
  • Optional steps can wait. Nominee and F&O income proof both have a clear “later” option.
  • I could start right away. A user ID arrived the moment I finished, and I could explore Kite while the application was under review.

If I were the PM

  1. An honest progress bar. Show time left, and group password and 2FA under “Finish setup” rather than after “Congratulations”. Mostly copy and layout.
  2. One place to sign up. Keep sign-up inside the app, and give the e-sign hand-off a sentence of explanation. The most work of the three, and it removes the most drop-off points.
  3. A beginner-safe default. Start with stocks and mutual funds, and offer F&O later. A small screen change with a real protective effect.

To measure them, I'd track completion at every screen, so drop-off at each hand-off shows up, plus the median time from opening the app to the first watchlist. As a guardrail, I'd watch the share of new users who switch on F&O in their first week, since fewer is the intended effect of fix 3, not a loss.

Method and limits: one person on one run, by someone who already uses fintech apps, so a nervous first-time investor likely feels more friction. Timings come from the screenshots' clock. Screenshots aren't published because they contain personal details.
