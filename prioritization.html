Prioritization: 10 ideas for Fold, by impact and effort

I'd build three quick wins first: the bank-sync alert, tips that point users to features Fold already has, and manually added accounts. All three come straight from Fold's reviews, and each is a few weeks of work, not a quarter.

  • Question

    With ten credible ideas from users, what should Fold build first?

  • Answer

    Three quick wins now; plan smarter auto-tagging as the one big bet.

  • Caveat

    Effort is my estimate from outside. It's a starting point for a conversation with engineering.

At a glance

The top three

  1. Bank-sync alert. Every sync review in the sample was serious, and tracking is the only thing Fold sells, so a silently stale account breaks the core promise. Fold already tracks each bank's last refresh; the gap is putting that date on the balances and totals people actually read. See the PRD and A/B plan.
  2. Tips for features Fold already has. Five reviewers asked for things that exist: salary-cycle months, manual entries, adding property, repayments, and tag rules for repeat merchants. A hint at the moment of need costs little and turns complaints into features users didn't know they had.
  3. Manually added accounts. Some money can't come through Account Aggregator, such as payments banks and banks whose feeds keep failing. Letting users add an account and update its balance by hand closes that gap, and builds on manual entries Fold already supports.
The big bet to plan next: smarter auto-tagging.Wrong categories hit Fold's core job hardest of any theme, but it is the most work by far, because it needs learning across users. It shows in my own data too: in my October breakdown, Untagged was my largest spending category. I prototyped one approach.

How I scored them

Impact (1 to 5) weighs how many reviews raised the issue, how badly it hurts (blocks use, shows wrong numbers, or just annoys), and how close it sits to Fold's core job of accurate tracking. Effort (1 to 5) is my estimate from outside: I can't see Fold's code.

Two limits: reviews over-represent unhappy users, and one review can't show how common a request is. The ranking should be re-checked against Fold's own usage data.
