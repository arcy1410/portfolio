Work / Amazon redesign

Amazon's product page, built for deciding

Amazon's product page makes it easy to buy and hard to decide. On a ₹529 sling bag, Buy Now appears on the third screen, after six price and urgency messages. The reviews that flag a weak lock and a disputed waterproof claim appear on the eleventh. My redesign puts the decision and the Buy button on the first screen.

Surface
Amazon India app, product page
Evidence
15 screenshots, 24 blocks
Deliverable
Teardown and redesign
Date
31 Oct 2026

  • Problem

    Every block serves a team's clicks; nobody owns the shopper's decision.

  • Redesign

    Screen 1 answers: is it good, what does it cost, when will it arrive. Nothing is deleted; the rest moves lower.

  • Test

    Purchases must hold; returns should fall; ad revenue is the guardrail.

A concept for a portfolio, not affiliated with Amazon. Screenshots are mine, from one product page; my delivery address is hidden.

Today's page

I mapped every block on the page in the order a shopper scrolls past it.

The problem

  1. Another seller's bag comes first. The first block, above even the product title, is a sponsored carousel for a different brand.
  2. Prices and urgency crowd the Buy button. Before Buy Now, a shopper meets five different numbers: ₹529, an M.R.P. of ₹1,999, ₹504 with UPI, ₹26 cashback and ₹250 back on ₹2,500, plus two timers. Even the cashback disagrees with itself: ₹25 in one block, ₹26 in the next.
  3. What decides the purchase comes last. The title says waterproof. The review summary says the lock and zips get mixed reviews, and a top review says the bag isn't waterproof as written. Both sit at the bottom, after seven sponsored blocks.

My reading of why: each block likely belongs to a different team (ads, payments, deals, recommendations), and each optimizes its own clicks. Nobody owns the shopper's decision as a whole.

Five changes

  1. Review summary moves up, from screen 11 to screen 1, with the weak spots shown, not hidden.
  2. Buy Now moves up, from screen 3 to screen 1.
  3. Four offers become one line. UPI cashback, ₹26 back, ₹250 on ₹2,500 and GST pricing sit behind one tap.
  4. One time cue instead of two. The deal countdown goes; the delivery cutoff stays, because it's a real fact.
  5. Sponsored blocks move below reviews. All seven stay on the page, lower down.

The biggest change is honesty: showing the weak spots up front costs a few impulse sales, but should cut returns.

Trade-offs, and how I'd test it

Amazon earns money from every block I'm moving, so this can't be a pure design call. Sponsored placements are ad revenue, and the UPI offer grows Amazon Pay. I'd run it as an A/B test on product pages in one category before going wider.

MetricRoleWhy
Purchases per product-page viewPrimaryThe redesign has to sell at least as well
Return rate within 10 daysSecondaryShoppers who see the weak spots first should buy the right thing more often
Time from page open to purchaseSecondaryLess scrolling should mean faster decisions
Ad revenue per page viewGuardrailSponsored blocks move lower, so this may fall
UPI payment setupsGuardrailThe UPI offer moves behind one tap

If purchases hold and returns fall, the lower ad revenue is a cost worth debating, because fewer returns save money on every order.

Limits: this rests on one product page, seen on one phone during a festival sale. Amazon personalizes and tests its layouts, so other shoppers may see a different page.
