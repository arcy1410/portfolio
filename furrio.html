Furrio: taking a pet-supplies reseller online, on every channel

Furrio sells dog and cat supplies from brands like Trixie, KONG and Ruffwear, and until now only through Amazon and Flipkart. As a freelance project, I set up its own store with cash on delivery, online payments, shipping and stock, built so the founder can run it alone.

The short version

  • Problem

    Furrio sold only through Amazon and Flipkart, with no store or customers of its own.

  • Build

    A Shopify store with 308 products, cash on delivery and online payments, Shiprocket shipping and one stock count.

  • Result

    Launched after an 18-point check of the live store, with every price rule tested in the real checkout.

Brief

What the client needed

A one-person reseller needed a store she could run herself, next to the marketplaces she already sells on.

  • She resells, she doesn't make300-plus products from brands like Trixie, KONG and Ruffwear, so the catalogue had to be built from product data, photos included.
  • Cash on delivery is expectedMany Indian shoppers still pay at the door, so COD had to work from day one, without losing money on it.
  • She runs it aloneSo I kept every tool free or low-cost, and every daily task doable from her phone.
  • The marketplaces stayAmazon and Flipkart keep selling; the store adds a channel she owns. Quick commerce was ruled out.

Decision

One business, four ways to buy

Each channel gets one clear owner for payment, delivery and stock, so nothing is entered twice.

Free shipping from ₹499 gives small carts a reason to grow; below it, ₹49 covers the courier. The ₹50 fee applies only to cash on delivery, which costs more to collect and is refused at the door more often, so paying online is the cheaper choice.

Build

What I set up

Five pieces, each checked on the live store before I called it done.

  1. Catalogue308 products and 518 options imported by spreadsheet, a photo for every one, and 10 categories, Dogs, Cats and 8 brand pages that fill themselves from product data.
  2. StorefrontA theme in Furrio’s palette, original illustrations, 12 category icons, a logo and favicon, and a link-preview image.
  3. CheckoutCash on delivery through EasySell with the shipping and fee rules, Razorpay for paying online, and a branded checkout.
  4. ShippingShiprocket for courier pickup and tracking, matching the 7–10 day delivery promise in the shipping policy.
  5. StockShopify’s own counts for the website, so 0 shows Sold out, plus a private tracker for marketplace sales.

Decision

Three palettes before the right one

The first two each looked like a company people already know, so I changed the approach, not just the colours.

  • DroppedTeal and orangeRead as Apollo 24|7, a pharmacy brand.
  • DroppedMango and plumRead as a bank: orange and maroon on every bar and button.
  • LiveLinen to EspressoFive warm neutrals; the colour comes from the illustrations and the products.

Both rejects failed the same way: two strong brand colours on every bar and button is how banks and pharmacy chains colour-code. The fix was structural. Neutrals carry the structure, and the products bring the colour. I redrew the hero art, the icons and the logo in the five colours, and tried the final palette on the real homepage in my browser before it went live.

Decision

Where stock lives: I built more, then shipped less

I built a stock tracker and a two-way Shopify sync, then chose not to ship the sync.

First I built Furrio a private stock tracker: a two-person web app with sales by channel and a monthly view. When the founder wanted the website to show Sold out on its own, there were two ways to do it. One was to sync the tracker to Shopify, with counts pushed on every change and website orders flowing back. I wrote that sync. The other was to keep website stock in Shopify itself.

  • Why Shopify's own countsOne source of truth for the website, nothing that can drift or break, no keys to maintain, and the free Shopify app on her phone.
  • What the tracker still doesMarketplace and offline sales, with the monthly view by channel. The sync is parked, ready for when Amazon and Flipkart need to share one count.

Next

What I’d measure after launch

Four numbers tell Furrio whether the rules are paying off.

  • Share of website orders paid onlineThe ₹50 COD fee should push this up; if it doesn’t, the fee is only friction.
  • COD orders refused at the doorReturned parcels cost shipping both ways; a confirm-by-WhatsApp step is the first fix to try.
  • Average order value near ₹499If carts bunch just above the free-shipping line, the threshold is working.
  • Website share of all salesFrom the tracker’s channel view, against Amazon and Flipkart, month by month.
